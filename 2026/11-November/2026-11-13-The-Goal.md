Just finished reading The Goal by Eliyahu Goldratt, the book from 1984 that taught the world the Theory of Constraints. A concept that lives on today in Agile, Lean, Scrum, anything that deals with WIP.

It’s a fun book that is presented as a novel with our hero Alex who has to try and turn around a failing manufacturing plant. (Spoiler alert, he did)

From the Theory of Constraints Institute:
“The Goal book is relevant for all types of situations because it is about learning what makes the world tick so that you can improve it. As the characters "think logically about their problems" they gradually uncover the "cause and effect relationships" between actions and results. 

The Goal is one of three books that Jeff Bezos requires his top management team to read. In fact, he uses it as a framework for sketching out the future of Amazon.com.”

If you read Gene Kim’s The Phoenix Project, this book shares the same kind of vibe and topics. Gene even wrote that his book was modeled after it, nearly 30 years after The Goal was written.

From IT Revolution:
“Charlie Betz told me that it was like a “slasher flick for IT,” resembling Stephen King more than our mutual hero Dr. Eliyahu Goldratt, who wrote the book, “The Goal,” which our book is definitely modeled after.  In fact, each of the main characters of the book are a synthesis of many real people, which is why I believe they resonate so well with readers.”

By the way if you haven’t heard of IT Revolution before… oh my gosh. Just go read everything there. Every publication, every book… it’s amazing.

And also read The Goal. Even if you’re not in manufacturing, it’s a quick read and helps you think differently about problem solving. The audio book version is good too.
