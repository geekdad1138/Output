Should all AIs have the same access as native system AIs? Apple's Siri and Google's Gemini have deeply integrated ties into the operating system. And now the EU is claiming that gives them an unfair advantage against other AIs from companies like OpenAI or Anthropic.

This feels a lot like the browser wars of decades ago, when browser makers were fighting for dominance over the desktop. But now it's a new arena, the dominance over your smart phone.

From the EU's press release, Teresa Ribera Executive Vice-President for Clean, Just and Competitive Transition said:
"Society is going through a profound digital transformation. We need to keep that process fair and ensure that our citizens have choice. Our decision will help smaller competitors, search engines, or AI assistants, to compete and provide that choice, while protecting the user’s privacy."

And Henna Virkkunen, Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy said:
"With today’s measures, we want to support innovation and diversity in the European Union, enabling fair competition in the markets of AI assistant for Android devices and search engines. Thanks to these measures we hope to see emerging alternatives to Google Search and Google’s AI services, such as Gemini, and that users in the EU can enjoy greater choice of services. All developers, large and small, are welcome to explore these new opportunities, which will certainly benefit users too."

Kent Walker, Google and Alphabet's president of global affairs said the changes would "pose a major security risk".

Apple went so far as to pull back the launch of their new Siri updates because of this mandate.

The market for AI assistants on smartphones is a huge space with multi-billion dollar companies all fighting to be the app you choose. ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Deepseek, Perplexity, Vibe(Mistral), Meta, Poe, Kimi, Grok and more are all out there living in that restricted secured third party space, whether that's a good or bad thing.

Even Google's Gemini would get granted deeply integrated access over Apple's devices, giving them the possibility to monopolize the entire smartphone market.

It's an incredibly important space in the future of privacy and control. Whether Apple and Google get to have special secret access, or open it up to others. And when, not if, there is a security issue questions arise about whether Apple/Google should be held responsible in any way.
