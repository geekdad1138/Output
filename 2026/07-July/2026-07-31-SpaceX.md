SpaceX just launched Space Robots to help fix up satellites in orbit! The MRV-MEP was launched into GEO on the B1069 Falcon 9 rocket last week for the NASA/DARPA RSGS, and equipped with a PRM for refueling.

That’s a lot of acronyms to chew through!

Basically it’s a satellite with 10 foot robotic arms. It flies around and attaches jetpacks to old satellites.

Those jetpacks then help keep the old satellites in the right orbit, up to 8 years longer.

And the robot satellite can be refueled in space.

Now for all the acronyms:

- MRV: Mission Robotic Vehicle. This is the satellite with 10 foot robotic arms that flies around and attaches the jet packs to old satellites.
- MEP: Mission Extension Pods. These are the jet packs that get attached to old satellites. They use electric propulsion to slowly push the satellites where they need to be in space.
- GEO: Geostationary Orbit. 22,236 miles above the earth. At this height the satellites stay in the same spot above the earth rotating with it at the same speed.
- B1069 Falcon 9: The Falcon 9 is a specific model of SpaceX rocket, and the B1069 is a specific Booster rocket that has done many missions.
- RSGS: Robotic Servicing of Geosynchronous Satellites. A DARPA program to help address the maintenance of satellites in orbit.
- PRM: Passive Refueling Module. Part of the MRV that allows it to be refueled in space.

This project had a huge number of groups involved to get it done.
- US Government agencies NASA - National Aeronautics and Space Administration, Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA), U.S. Naval Research Laboratory Lab, United States Space Force: Provided research, funding and facilities
- NGIS: Northrop Grumman Innovation Systems. Their SpaceLogistics group designed the MRV, MEP, and PRM.
- SpaceX: Provided the Falcon 9 rocket that launched it all into space.

And from the space.com article, the MRV is capable of even more:
“In addition to installing MEPs, the MRV can relocate, inspect, repair and upgrade spacecraft while in orbit”

It’s a massive project to help tackle a massive problem. Right now there are thousands of old unused satellites. Some that fell out of GEO, or others that just broke down.

Putting space robots in orbit seems like a cool way to help.
