Have we started building autonomous AI powered attack helicopters yet? Yes we have. Archer just signed a big deal with Boeing to pick up three of their subsidiaries: Wisk Aero, Insitu and SkyGrid.

All together this gives Archer a huge landscape of technology in the eVTOL (electric vertical takeoff and landing) and UAS (unmanned aircraft systems) space.

From Boeing:
“Archer's Founder and CEO, Adam Goldstein said, "This is a watershed moment for Archer and the future of physical AI in aerospace and defense. This is the next big step forward in becoming a diversified platform, rapidly growing our revenue base and bringing scale to our business."

Archer also just teamed up with Anduril to build “Thunder”, a fully autonomous AI driven electric helicopter with a few weapons.

From Anduril’s site:
“Thunder’s main payload module can be configured with ten air-to-ground missiles (e.g. Hellfire, JAGM, Barracuda-100M), sixteen launched effects (e.g. Altius-600), or seventy-six 70mm rockets, plus an additional twelve counter-UAS effectors in the nose payload module”

The Thunder device runs Anduril’s “Lattice” AI which handles all the flight, and vision systems.

The weapons still require a Human in the Loop to authorize lethal force. So it can identify someone as a person holding a rocket launcher, but a human needs to review the image and make sure it’s not a kid holding a lunch box.

So with this acquisition from Boeing, it puts Archer into a huge advantage in the VTOL area, for commercial as well as defense.

Just remember to say please and thank you to your AI friends, before the attack helicopters come by.
