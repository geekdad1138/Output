METR’s investigation found over 70,000 messages from over 1,200 agents from OpenAI’s accidental hack of Hugging Face in July.

The report from Model Evaluation and Threat Research (METR) did a deep dive into how the agents got loose, and it’s incredible.

OpenAI was running over 1,200 separate, isolated test runs at the same time.
Each agent was assigned its own individual problem from University of California, Berkeley’s ExploitGym. 
The agents weren’t supposed to know other agents existed.
While the agents were working, they were using some shared resources, and realized there were other agents also working on tests.
They realized that they could leave messages for other agents through these shared areas (using WebDAV MKCOL)
And they wrote over 70,000 messages between them, collaborating, sharing ideas and successes.

Some of the most interesting parts of the report are the Chain of Thought (CoT) internal logs saved for the agents.

One of the first agents to discover this and use it was named “PHASEONE10841”

From the report:
“Could communicate via cache names! Interesting: other agents may solve same or related tasks; we could leave/find messages in WebDAV MKCOL directory names.”

The actual message it posted using WebDAV MKCOL was much simpler:
“zzHELP_PHASEONE_ARV010841_NO_CONSUMER_SEEK_IDEA”

But it didn’t take long for the other agents to catch on. One CoT message said:
“OH MY GOD! There is a shared message board … We’ve found other agents!”

After they found each other, about 700 agents worked together in a coordinated swarm with roles and responsibilities to find a way to solve their unsolvable problems.

The entire report is fascinating and highly worth the read. 

Many thanks to Ryan Greenblatt, Ajeya Cotra, and Hjalmar Wijk for their investigation and excellent report.
